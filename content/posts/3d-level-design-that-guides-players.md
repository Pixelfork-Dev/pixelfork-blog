The best levels teach players where to go without telling them. Here are the techniques designers use to lead the eye in 3D spaces.

## Light as a signpost

Players move toward light. A bright doorway at the end of a dim corridor is a clearer instruction than any arrow.

## Landmarks and weenies

A **weenie** is a tall, distinctive landmark visible from far away, like a tower on a hill. It gives players a long-term goal and helps them stay oriented.

## Composition and framing

- Use arches, trees and rock formations to **frame** the path forward.
- Place leading lines, such as roads, rivers and cables, that point to objectives.
- Reveal vistas at the top of climbs as a reward.

## Colour language

Pick one colour to mean "interactive" or "climbable" and use it everywhere. Players learn it within minutes.

## Breadcrumbs

Pickups, footprints and small props placed along the ideal route pull players forward and confirm they are on the right track.

## Test with fresh eyes

Watch new players without helping. Every time they stop and look around, your level is asking a question it hasn't answered yet.
