Launch day is too late to start marketing. The games that break out usually spent months building an audience first.

## Share development early

Post short clips of gameplay, bugs and before-and-after comparisons. **Short videos under 15 seconds** perform best on X, TikTok, Instagram and YouTube Shorts.

## Keep a devlog

A regular devlog, whether written or on video, gives followers a reason to come back and gives search engines pages to index.

## Build a community home

Start a Discord server or subreddit once you have a small following. Share builds with members first. They become your testers and your loudest advocates.

## Prepare a press kit

Include:

- A one-paragraph description
- A trailer and 5–10 high-resolution screenshots
- Logos and key art
- Release date, platforms and price
- Contact details

## Reach out to creators

Find YouTubers and streamers who cover games like yours. Send a short, personal email with a key and a clear reason your game fits their channel.

## Measure what works

Track wishlists, follows and clicks for every post. Double down on the channels that move numbers and drop the ones that don't.

> Marketing isn't a phase after development. It's a habit that runs alongside it.
