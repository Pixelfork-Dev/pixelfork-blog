The best monetization strategy is the one your players barely notice. Choose the wrong model, though, and even a great game can stall. Here is how the main options compare.

## The three core models

### 1. Ad-supported (free)

Revenue comes from interstitial, banner and **rewarded video** ads. Rewarded ads, where players opt in for a bonus, get the best feedback from players and usually earn the most per impression.

### 2. In-app purchases (free-to-play)

Players buy cosmetics, currency, level packs or remove-ads upgrades. A small share of players generates most of the revenue, so your economy needs careful design.

### 3. Premium (paid up front)

Players pay once. It's simple and player-friendly, but discovery is harder when there is a price tag.

## Choosing a model

| Game type | Good fit |
| --- | --- |
| Hyper-casual, short sessions | Ads, mostly rewarded |
| Mid-core with progression | IAP plus rewarded ads |
| Narrative or puzzle, finite content | Premium or a paid unlock |

## Rules that protect retention

- **Never interrupt the core loop.** Show interstitials at natural breaks, never mid-level.
- **Make rewards generous.** A rewarded ad should feel like a good deal.
- **Offer a remove-ads purchase.** Your most engaged players will often buy it.
- **Measure everything.** Track day-1 and day-7 retention next to ARPDAU. If revenue goes up but retention drops, you've borrowed from the future.

## A simple starting point

For most first-time indie mobile games, start with **rewarded ads plus a single remove-ads IAP**. It's easy to build, fair to players, and gives you data before you design a deeper economy.
